# Essay 07 revision notes

## Panel-review decisions

- One workload model now governs the economics: six supplier delivery days per active account per year, including onboarding and routine operations. Buyer value, not supplier effort, supports the €24,000–€30,000 recurring price and determines the experiment's value-case kill condition.
- Mature delivery arithmetic: 20 accounts × 6 days + 8 diagnostics × 4 days + 12 specialist days = 164 days. At the €800 loaded-day design hurdle plus €30,000 infrastructure, delivery cost is €161,200, rounded to €161,000. A 300-day pool less 84 reserved days leaves 216 account days, or 36 accounts at the ceiling.
- Mature cohort: 20 opening + 8 added − 8 churned = 20 closing, with 20 average active accounts. Recurring revenue is therefore 20 × 12 × €2,500 = €600,000.
- Customer twenty is twenty screening-provider legal entities introduced through three to five channel partners. Each screener signs, pays, registers, controls evidence and handles exceptions. The operator is the intermediary. Channel partners earn 15% of onboarding and recurring fees but do not enter the wallet flow.
- The Article 5b(10) architecture is deliberately conservative. Transaction evidence goes directly to a screener-controlled store. The operator's accumulating asset is conformance tooling, synthetic fixtures, adapters and failure taxonomy, not applicant history.
- Dutch counsel remains necessary to classify correlation identifiers, transaction-linked policy references and support logs, and to reassess any topology in which an employer or channel partner requests wallet data for its own service.
