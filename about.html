Method

Five ways to earn the next experiment

This book adapts the sequence from The Going Concern: demand, payment, economics, operations and resilience. It adds a sixth question because the reader wants a company rather than only a well-paid practice: what, exactly, scales?

The reader held constant

The comparison assumes an experienced enterprise full-stack developer, approximately forty, living in Zaandam, with a Dutch passport, A2 Dutch and willingness to improve, up to EUR 100,000 available, and readiness to travel and do physical work. The target is at least EUR 100,000 annual owner income on a credible three-to-five-year path. Ordinary living expenses are not silently treated as business capital.

The sequence

  1. Demand: a named buyer faces a costly event. A rule or trend by itself is insufficient.
  2. Payment: the buyer has authority and a reason to rank this purchase above other work.
  3. Economics: gross margin pays delivery labour at replacement cost, operating expenses, working capital and the owner.
  4. Operations: the first promise can be delivered repeatedly and safely.
  5. Resilience: an incumbent response, standards change or lost partner does not erase the business overnight.
  6. Scalability: acquisition repeats, delivery is teachable, value genuinely recurs and an asset accumulates.

What counts as evidence

Laws and deadlines come from EUR-Lex, the European Commission, governments and regulators. Technical requirements come from standards bodies, official guidance and product documentation. Competitors are described from their own current materials, with marketing claims labelled as such. Every material current claim in an essay has a nearby marker and a row in its claim ledger.

Economic tables are usually models, not forecasts. They expose the price, customer count, margin and staffing the business would require. Those assumptions become interview and paid-pilot questions; they do not become true because the arithmetic balances.

The capable failure

Book 4 used counter-cases: competent people follow apparently sound advice and still lose. New markets do not always offer a clean historical case, so each essay constructs the strongest evidence-compatible path by which capable execution fails. Standards can settle around somebody else's architecture. A channel can learn the work and remove its supplier. A repeatable demo can become bespoke at installation. The failure must attack the idea, not lazily blame sales or execution.

The verdicts

Each essay ends with one of five provisional labels: TEST NOW, TEST THROUGH A PARTNER, WATCH, ACQUIRE, DO NOT BUILD, or DO NOT ENTER. None means invest EUR 100,000. The first commitment is a bounded paid experiment with a written pass and kill rule.
