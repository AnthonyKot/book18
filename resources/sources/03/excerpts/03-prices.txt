The first offer is CodeProof, a fixed €3,500 feasibility and loss diagnostic. This is a required test price, not an observed market price.

Every number below is a hurdle, not a claimed Dutch turnkey market price.

All volumes, prices and costs are designed arithmetic, not market facts.

The working-capital rule is 50% of the company’s wholesale price at order, 40% after factory acceptance and 10% after site acceptance. A €17,500 deposit almost covers the assumed €18,000 hardware and assembly bill for one cell; two overlapping builds still require cash. After evidence, the maximum €100,000 allocation is €28,000 for a pre-production/demo cell and test packs, €32,000 working capital, €15,000 for insurance/legal/conformity documentation, €8,000 already spent on discovery and €17,000 contingency. No inventory beyond one critical-spares kit is justified until three paid installations pass.

Factory acceptance requires 250 holdout images for every defect-class and declared-condition combination, plus 1,500 good images per condition. Site acceptance runs 100 independently prepared challenge packs for every combination through trigger, tracking and confirmed reject. A 10,000-good-pack run spans all conditions without bypass.

The gates are zero false accepts and a false-reject upper bound no higher than 0.2%. These samples are design hurdles, not proof of zero field risk.

Customer one consumes the founder. A rented camera and two lights go to the lab.

Customer five gets one of two standard conveyor widths, a fixed camera-to-pack envelope, prewired cabinet, versioned PLC handshakes for the two PLC families actually encountered, and a commissioning fixture that can replay triggers.

Customer twenty arrives through a partner’s installed base.

At 36 supported lines, one support engineer plus partner first response is a design assumption to measure.

The partner keeps roughly one-third of the end price in the hurdle model.

The team executes well. Two cheese packers pay for diagnostics. The optical design passes on glossy film, the reject path survives site acceptance, operators like the evidence log, and a respected Dutch integrator sells five cells.

Five conversations with no dated event end the segment test early.

Offer Vision Partners first—then at most one other integrator—registered-account protection, 20% of diagnostics, a target wholesale schedule and first-line ownership. No signed experiment by day 30 means no equipment purchase.

Run ten event interviews.

Sell two CodeProof diagnostics at €3,500 each. Quote the €52,000 cell only after the defect and integration envelope is known.

Spend at most €8,000 before evidence. Pass only if one integrator signs the channel experiment, two independent packers pay full diagnostic price, at least one line meets the optical test, and one buyer gives a conditional order at the price hurdle subject to site acceptance. Kill if any element is absent by day 90, if more than two optical/mechanical variants are needed, or if the partner will not leave the company the modelled margin and data rights.
