Book 18 · Seven options under uncertainty

The Second Engine

You have a career that still works and a reason not to bet your next decade on it. These essays do not predict the death of software development. They ask a narrower question: which modern technical business deserves the next two years and the first euro of a bounded investment?

The ruleA candidate must survive demand, payment, economics, operations and resilience—in that order. Novelty is not demand. Regulation is not a budget. Revenue is not owner income. Automation is not yet a scalable company.

Portfolio readingCandidate 1 is the only direct test because it has a dated queue, an English-capable cross-border buyer and a low-cost route to paid evidence. Candidates 2–5 require a partner because the missing capability is not primarily software: it is distribution, domain authority, plant access, field installation or regulatory interpretation. Do not run five builds. Run buyer discovery in parallel, then fund at most the first experiment that reaches its payment gate.

The prices and mature economics are design hurdles, not market averages or forecasts. Every verdict remains provisional until an unrelated buyer pays and the recurring work survives delivery.
