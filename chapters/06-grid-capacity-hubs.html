Essay 6 of 7 · Grid-congestion Capacity Control for Business Parks

A shared limit is a real operating system, not an energy dashboard

A factory that cannot electrify a process, add a charger depot or energise an expansion has a purchase event; a sustainability officer does not. Dutch energy hubs can now apply for a group transport agreement, but grid operators do not have to offer one until 2027. The opportunity is a control-and-evidence layer that keeps a qualified group inside its shared limit and explains every intervention. The fatal possibility is that the installer, trader, aggregator or grid operator already owns the controls, hardware and Dutch-language relationship. This is not a thesis about selling optimisation software to every business park. It tests whether one blocked site will buy a neutral operating layer through the party it already trusts.

The proposition

The first customer is a group of four to eight large connections behind one eligible electricity station in Liander territory. It is an existing business park where transport capacity blocks one member's signed expansion, electrified fleet, cold store or production equipment. The economic buyer is that member's operations or investment director, with park management as organiser. The group, not an individual sustainability lead, must choose who may use shared headroom and who pays when it is scarce.

The product is a fixed-scope capacity operating baseline, followed by a managed control-and-evidence service. It reconciles revenue-meter and controller readings against the group limit, member allocations and operating state. It can issue a proposed curtailment order and record the command, acknowledgement, response and override. A monthly report explains exceptions. The company does not trade energy, sell a battery, procure a grid connection, promise capacity, or sign the group's transport contract.

The practical boundary is important. A group transport agreement shares transport capacity; it does not make unrelated sites one electrical installation. At Enexis, each participant retains its own connection, the group has one transport contract, and an energy-management system is required to apply the allocation and remain within the group capacity. The business can connect to an installer's battery, charger, building-management or process controller, but it must not take over safety interlocks or process sequencing. The equipment owner retains final local authority.

The substitute is substantial: a park manager's spreadsheet and group chat; an installer-supplied energy-management system; a trader or aggregator's virtual power plant; a consultant's feasibility model; or a member choosing a smaller project and keeping its individual limit. The proposed layer wins only when it makes the shared promise more operable and auditable than those substitutes. A prettier load chart does not justify a new supplier.

What changed

The group contract has moved from proposal to code, but availability still needs qualification. On 19 December 2025, ACM said energy-hub companies can apply and grid operators must offer the form from 1 January 2027. The legal route is live; a universal service obligation is not. Neither fact guarantees a useful group limit or approves a control vendor.

Implementation is uneven. Liander describes a current group-contract application route for large connections above 3×80 A, requiring a formed hub, high-frequency metering and all members on the same electricity station. It says at least one member must have contracted transport capacity and gives an average two-year lead time for forming the group and agreeing the contract. Enexis says it is working with ten business parks and expects to offer group transport agreements (GTOs, after the Dutch term) more broadly from 2027. An entrant should therefore sell neither “a Dutch GTO product” nor a deadline. It should qualify the operator, station topology, connection class and application status before a software proposal.

Other contract forms solve different operating problems. In a decision announced on 19 July 2024, ACM allowed regional operators to offer time-block transport from 1 April 2025. It also described a TenneT duration-bound right that permits maximum use at least 85% of the time. ACM's 2 June 2026 dashboard separately lists capacity-restriction and capacity-steering contracts, group variants, time-block and duration-bound rights, and a fully non-firm right tied to actual grid loading.

The difference between code and product is visible at Enexis. Its Blokstroom application route for consumption started on 1 April 2025. Its fully variable Reststroom is still under investigation because available capacity cannot yet be seen well enough. A project needing a firm evening peak cannot be sold either form and called solved.

Capacity-limiting contracts are also concrete. Liander describes a CBC with call-up, a CBC with fixed adjustment times, and redispatch in which a Congestion Service Provider (CSP) bids the customer's flexibility on the GOPACS trading platform. An ACM dashboard defines a CBC more narrowly: a company voluntarily and temporarily adjusts load when overload threatens, in exchange for compensation. That duty and payment do not prove that a park will fund a separate evidence layer.

The public response is material but is not customer revenue. Rijksoverheid says €62 million is available for advice and investments for organisations blocked by a full grid, and €166 million for stimulating energy hubs. Those are programme budgets, not a price list, award to this company, or proof that an installer will share margin. The demand evidence that matters is narrower: a member has a blocked, costed investment and the group has reached a contract and governance choice that must be operated.

The first sale

Use an actual operator map to build a ten-type prospect list. Include park-management foundations, estates with an approved hub study, and cold-storage, food, logistics, metals, waste and fleet-depot anchor tenants. Include the electrical installer or energy adviser already analysing their profiles. Screen out parks without common station eligibility, a committed anchor investment, or agreement to share interval data. An ambitious solar plan is not enough.

The opening route is through a Dutch-speaking electrical installer and an energy consultant, not independent English-language outbound. The required partner role includes explaining switchgear, meter access, permits, operating limits and member politics. The founder can lead integration design and a transparent evidence model in English; the channel partner must lead Dutch workshops, contractual discovery and on-call discussions. If the partner asks the founder to become the park's Dutch-speaking energy manager, the model violates the reader's constraint and should be declined.

Sell a four-week, €6,000 paid capacity operating baseline. That is a required test price, not an observed market rate. It reconciles fifteen-minute data against the group's proposed limit; maps controllable loads and their local owners; rehearses one safe curtailment path in a test window; specifies the command/acknowledgement/evidence log; and produces an exception and liability schedule. It excludes hardware, switchgear changes, protection settings, a grid-operator application, energy-market trading and any promise that the hub will be admitted.

The signers should be the anchor member's investment director and the hub entity or delegated park manager, because a control order reallocates a shared commercial right. The production offer is a required hurdle of €20,000 onboarding plus €4,500 per live site per month, billed through the installer under a disclosed wholesale schedule. Hardware and installation remain on the installer's invoice. Before proposing it, require a written allocation policy, a 24/7 contact at every member and a controller test interface. The group must also decide who bears the consequence when a member overrides a curtailment instruction.

Does the money work?

This is design arithmetic, not a survey of Dutch prices. Every price, conversion and cost below is a validation hurdle. The model deliberately keeps batteries, meters, gateways, electrical works and installation off this company's revenue and warranty line. Baselines carry no installer discount. Year one pays replacement-cost founder labour but no owner distribution. It loses €89,250. Adding a two-month cash-cost buffer and €5,000 capex puts peak cash at about €124,750. The €100,000 capital ceiling therefore fails by €24,750 under these hurdles. The ninety-day €15,000 cap must sit inside these annual costs, not on top. Continue only if signed terms, volume or lower delivery effort close the gap before hiring.

The mature case pays €110,000 for the founder's operating role and a separate €100,000 owner distribution. It retains €191,500 before tax. After a €75,800 working-capital reserve and €10,000 capex, €105,700 remains. The 67% gross margin is not licence economics. It funds integration variance, secure telemetry, control testing, audit records, specialist review and response cover.

Two delivery engineers must each run 7.5 sites as a design hurdle. They handle integrations, tested commands, exceptions, overrides and incident reviews, with an electrical-control specialist covering escalations. If each peak needs founder judgement, the margin is fictional. The installer cannot simply take whatever share it requests: each extra discount point reduces receipts while delivery duties remain.

Working capital is cash held, not an expense. The hurdle is two months of delivery and operating costs because collections or acceptance can still slip despite required terms. Production customers fund site hardware directly, pay onboarding 50% in advance and pay recurring service monthly in advance. Do not hire a second engineer, buy a gateway fleet or promise response times before signed receipts fund them.

From service to company

Customer one is a site study with a thin control path. The founder traces meter identifiers, discovers whether a charger, battery or process load actually accepts an external setpoint, and watches a supervised test. The useful output is not a dashboard. It is a signed map from contractual constraint to meter, controller, local owner, safety boundary, permitted action, acknowledgement and recovery procedure.

Customer five uses a versioned connector kit for the few controller classes actually encountered, a standard allocation-policy schema and a repeatable test protocol. A trained implementation engineer can onboard data, perform reconciliation and produce an exception report. The electrical-control specialist approves control boundaries; the founder handles only unresolved integration and commercial escalation. The installer sells the baseline from a fixed scope, not from a fresh technical essay.

Customer twenty is possible only through three to five enabled installer/adviser partners. Recurrence follows changing state. Members join or leave, allocations change, equipment is maintained and controllers go offline. New loads change profiles, contract calls change permitted actions, overrides need investigation and group settlement needs evidence. The accumulating asset is a tested library of controller adapters, policy templates, simulator fixtures, failure modes and evidence records. The agreed contracts must leave raw interval data with the member or group.

The four tests are severe. Acquisition repeats only when partners originate qualified baselines without the founder's personal introductions. Delivery is teachable only when someone other than the founder can pass a simulated command-and-recovery test from the playbook. Value recurs only because allocations, equipment and constraints change. The asset accumulates only when the next compatible controller and agreement can be onboarded faster without exposing a prior site's data. If two of these fail, call it specialist integration work and price it accordingly; do not call it a software company.

Liability starts with the transport right, not the software warranty. Stedin says the group is jointly responsible for staying within capacity and must record its internal allocation in an agreement. It also says an EMS is needed. Under ACM's GTO code decision, every breach is reported to the group's representative. Two or more breaches may lead to suspension. A further breach after remedies may ultimately end the transport right.

The code does not say who reimburses a member whose investment stops after a controller failure. ACM declined to prescribe civil agreements between connected parties or groups and grid operators because it lacked authority to do so. Allocation among members, the installer and the software supplier is therefore a private-contract design question, not an ACM holding. The paid baseline must propose a fault allocation: the member bears deliberate overrides; the installer bears proven hardware or wiring faults; and the software supplier bears proven command or logging defects, subject to a negotiated cap. Lost production, safety and upstream communication need explicit treatment. A Dutch lawyer and insurer must test that schedule before live control.

The incumbent gets a turn

There is no empty category to enter. Sympower markets a stack that connects, aggregates, optimises and trades energy assets, including hardware or API interfaces, for commercial and industrial companies. Withthegrid markets Dutch local control strategies including static and dynamic limitations, grid-operator endpoints and peak shaving. Here peak shaving means using batteries to counter short load or generation spikes that would breach a consumption or feed-in limit. Those are vendor descriptions, not independent evidence of outcomes, but they show that control, integration and market access are marketed together.

Scholt Energy markets flexibility around renewable generation, storage and industrial consumption. It says its virtual power plant connects those assets to energy markets. Enervalis' accessible portal says only “Creating more value with energy.” That is too little own-material evidence to attribute a current control product, outcome or price. It is not proof that Enervalis has no such capability. It makes vendor discovery necessary before claiming differentiation.

The grid operators have their own hub programmes. Liander publishes the eligibility route and process. Enexis has ten development sites, expects wider availability in 2027 and requires an EMS. Stedin describes the same control need and offers a Netburenscan to identify possible group members. The operator cannot be displaced from capacity allocation. An installer can retain the controller, and a trader can bundle dispatch. A park can also buy a consultant and an EMS. Any of them can make a separate evidence layer look redundant.

The narrow response is neutrality between equipment and market counterparties, not superiority in dispatch. Keep an independent contract-to-control evidence record, support the awkward mixed fleet an installer inherited, and make the installer more credible with its end customer. The partner receives branded reports, first-line relationship ownership and a clear wholesale margin. The new company retains the reconciliation engine, test fixtures, secure audit trail and specialist control policy. If the partner demands the source, the customer data and unrestricted reuse while paying a thin fee, it is buying know-how to internalise.

The capable failure

The company executes well. It finds an eligible park, maps the limits correctly, conducts safe control tests, documents overrides and never breaches a contracted group limit. The anchor tenant gets its investment moving. The installer presents the result to members, owns the hardware warranty and becomes the person called at 02:00. At renewal, the group treats evidence as a project appendix and asks the installer to fold the monitoring into its maintenance agreement.

The installer then has the protocol, the controller mappings and a working relationship with the energy adviser. The trader offers dispatch and reporting as part of its flexibility contract. The GTO has made the operating problem clearer, but not proprietary. The evidence product has also made every exception legible enough for a competent integrator to copy. Customer count rises, yet each site demands partner-specific integration and the wholesale price falls. No sales failure occurred; the layer created value but did not control a budget, a contractual choke point or a learning asset that the channel could not reclaim.

The first ninety days

  1. Days 1–15: choose Liander territory only. Interview ten installer, energy-adviser, park-manager and anchor-member prospects. For each, capture the blocked investment, network operator, station topology, connection category, contract form considered, controller estate and decision owner. Spend no more than €2,000 on travel and Dutch technical assistance.
  2. Days 10–25: retain an independent Dutch electrical-control engineer for up to €3,500 to define the safety boundary, test witness requirements and the manual-fallback protocol. If no qualified partner will own local safety and switching authority, stop before building control software.
  3. Days 20–35: pay a Dutch energy-contract lawyer up to €3,500 to review one GTO or hub cooperation agreement and draft the allocation, override, evidence-access and liability schedule. Do not pretend a technical log substitutes for a member agreement.
  4. Days 30–55: build only a simulator, interval-data reconciler, policy store, command/acknowledgement log and monthly exception report. Cap build, secure hosting and test connectivity at €6,000. No production gateway fleet, optimiser, trading desk or battery purchase.
  5. Days 45–70: offer the same €6,000 paid baseline, 50% in advance, to five qualified installer-led groups. It is valid only where an anchor member will expose redacted interval data and a safe controller-test interface.
  6. Days 70–90: deliver one baseline and seek the stated €20,000 onboarding plus €4,500 monthly production hurdle through the installer. Total pre-evidence spend is capped at €15,000.

Pass: one qualified group pays the baseline and completes a witnessed safe-control rehearsal. It must then make one binding commitment: a production order at the €20,000 onboarding and €4,500 monthly retail hurdles, a non-refundable €10,000 onboarding deposit, or a paid €4,500 first month. A letter of interest or dated authority does not count. Written allocation and liability terms must accompany the commitment. Kill or downgrade: no paid baseline after five qualified offers; no eligible blocked investment; installer capture of all evidence and reusable work; or a paid baseline without a production commitment by day 90. The last outcome kills the recurring-company thesis and downgrades the offer to occasional integration consulting, if premium self-employment is acceptable.

Verdict

TEST THROUGH A PARTNER

The single assumption most likely to change the verdict is that an installer or energy adviser can sell this recurring layer separately to a blocked anchor tenant.
