Essay 2 of 7 · White-label Product Security for Engineering Consultancies

The channel owns the account—and can learn the work

A CE adviser or embedded-development firm already sits beside the machine builder when a product changes. Giving that partner a specialist product-security back office avoids the hardest cold sale and reduces Dutch-language dependence. It also gives away the customer, part of the price and most of the trust. The partner can blame the hidden supplier when a finding is wrong, keep it invisible when the work is good, and hire one engineer after the playbook becomes clear. White-label product security is attractive only if the back office improves the partner's economics while retaining a capability that is hard to internalise.

The proposition

The first buyer is not a manufacturer. It is the managing director or practice lead of a three-to-twenty-five-person Benelux CE/machinery-safety, embedded-software or industrial-automation consultancy. The purchase event is an existing client asking for CRA scope, SBOM, secure-development, vulnerability-handling or incident-readiness work that the partner cannot staff confidently. The partner wants to say yes without recruiting a senior product-security specialist before demand is repeatable.

The back office sells the partner a fixed wholesale delivery unit for one end-customer product family: evidence intake, firmware/SBOM analysis orchestration, threat and vulnerability triage, secure-development evidence mapping, a vulnerability-disclosure workflow, an incident tabletop, and a branded delivery pack in the partner's template. A continuation unit provides weekly vulnerability operations and quarterly evidence refresh. The partner scopes the broader engagement, owns the end-customer contract, leads meetings and invoices. The back office may join as an unnamed specialist or disclosed subcontractor according to the risk.

This is not a hidden law firm, certification body, 24/7 incident-response service, general penetration-testing shop or cheap engineering bench. Counsel owns legal interpretation. A notified body or laboratory owns independent assessment. The end manufacturer remains the decision-maker for product risk, support, fixes and filings. The back office supplies technical analysis, controlled process and evidence; it refuses contractual language that says “guarantees CRA compliance” or transfers the manufacturer's statutory role.

The substitute is the partner's senior consultant learning the work, subcontracting a known pentester case by case, reselling a product-security platform, or referring the whole opportunity to a larger specialist. The proposed company must be better than all four while preserving the partner's client relationship. That makes partner economics and trust part of the product, not commercial details to settle later.

What changed

The underlying deadline is real. CRA reporting duties begin on 11 September 2026 and the main requirements on 11 December 2027. Manufacturers face a 24-hour early warning and 72-hour notification for defined exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents. The Act also requires vulnerability handling through the support period, coordinated disclosure, product risk assessment and technical documentation that is updated where appropriate.

Those are manufacturer obligations. Importers or distributors become manufacturers when they place a product on the market under their own name or trademark or substantially modify it; a separate party making a substantial modification and remarketing the product also takes manufacturer obligations for the affected part or whole. A subcontract does not by itself turn the back office into the statutory manufacturer, and it does not remove the manufacturer's obligations. That is a legal boundary to have counsel encode in every statement of work, not a promise this essay can make for a particular engagement.

The practical workload is broad enough to make specialists useful. In a small 2024 qualitative study of industrial-equipment organisations, respondents highlighted secure-development governance, security updates, scarce expertise, vulnerability management and the notification clock; they were less worried by many individual technical controls than by organisational ownership and process. But all seven surveyed manufacturers were large and already had IEC 62443-aligned development lifecycles, and the sample also included five consultancies or partner organisations. It validates the kind of work, not willingness among small firms to buy it or among consultancies to resell it.

A channel architecture does exist in adjacent security. Hard2bit publicly offers white-label, co-branded and referral models in which the partner retains the contract and client relationship while the specialist operates behind it; it says margins are negotiated rather than published and that its certifications do not transfer to the reseller. That is the precise trust and credential boundary a product-security back office must handle. It is first-party marketing evidence that the model is offered, not evidence of partner profitability or retention.

There is also an exact product-security channel precedent, though not a small Benelux white-label proof. Cybellum runs a solution-provider programme around its platform. In one published partnership, Hitachi Solutions combined its consulting and PSIRT work with Cybellum vulnerability-management technology to offer product-lifecycle and CRA support; the vendors said the service included organisational consulting, product-design support and continuous vulnerability monitoring. The partnership proves that customer-owning consultancies can combine service with a specialist product-security engine. It does not show what a new, invisible labour provider can charge.

The distinction between regulation and paid demand is therefore severe. The CRA gives end manufacturers a reason to act. Existing partner programmes show that security is sometimes distributed through advisers. Neither proves that an engineering consultancy will surrender delivery margin, disclose client artefacts and accept dependency on an unknown back office. Only a partner-funded engagement can establish that demand.

The first sale

Approach ten partner types: machinery CE adviser; embedded-Linux developer; PLC/SCADA integrator; industrial IoT platform implementer; OT-security boutique; product test laboratory without security depth; technical-documentation firm; functional-safety consultancy; contract electronics designer; and maintenance/retrofit engineering firm. Exclude general MSPs at first: enterprise IT security is not the same delivery motion, and their buyers are often operators rather than product manufacturers.

The route is founder-to-practice-lead, preferably through counsel, an assessor or an embedded engineer who has seen both parties work. Send a one-page wholesale service card with inputs, exclusions, turnaround, wholesale price, suggested retail band, escalation path and a redacted sample. Offer a ninety-minute partner enablement session, then ask to price one live opportunity. Conferences and associations can find partners; they do not validate the model.

The first paid test is a €3,000 partner rehearsal. Using a product the partner owns or has explicit permission to share, both teams run intake, classification boundaries, evidence handling, one vulnerability triage and a mock client readout. The fee is credited against the first wholesale delivery. This prevents months of “strategic partnership” meetings without a customer or money.

The wholesale hypothesis for the first live product baseline is €7,000. The partner is expected to retail it around €12,000 while providing sales, account knowledge, onsite discovery and final presentation. The recurring wholesale unit is €2,400 a month against an illustrative €4,000 end-customer price. Those are models, not observed prices. They intentionally leave the partner roughly 40% of end price before its delivery cost. If the partner cannot see at least that margin, it has no reason to introduce the supplier.

The initial contracting position is disclosed subcontracting for any assessment that could materially affect product release or a regulatory filing. Pure white-label is reserved for bounded evidence preparation and routine analysis after the process is proven. The partner remains the single commercial contact, but technical escalations must permit controlled direct access to the manufacturer's engineers. If a partner demands complete invisibility and filters every question, decline: hidden telephone chains turn product facts into liability.

English delivery can work inside export-oriented engineering teams, while the partner owns Dutch sales, workshops and end-customer documentation. That is a material advantage for the Zaandam-based founder. It is also dependence: the company cannot replace a Dutch-speaking partner quickly after a dispute. The first tests should include one Dutch-led and one cross-border English-led partner to learn whether language or specialist credibility actually drives the channel.

Does the money work?

Year one does not fund the owner target. The mature model does, but its apparent scale hides two ratios. Eight producing partners must each create four baseline projects a year. Three quarters of those baselines must convert or coexist as twenty-four recurring end customers. A partner that signs an agreement but produces no paid work is not a channel and does not belong in the denominator.

The direct-cost line allows four loaded delivery roles plus specialist review and tooling. Founder compensation of €105,000 is priced as replacement labour, not surplus. The €255,200 remainder is pre-tax company surplus for reserves, hiring and investment. Recurring revenue is €691,200, 76% of the total, but concentration matters more: if two partners source half the clients, they can renegotiate the wholesale rate or threaten to move the book.

Partner payment terms can create a cash gap even when the end customer prepays. If the partner pays at 45 or 60 days while staff are monthly, the mature operation can need more than €100,000 of payroll and tax liquidity. The launch terms must therefore prohibit “pay when paid,” require the booking deposit, stop work on overdue invoices and keep laboratory spend outside the company. Do not use the reader's capital to finance a better-funded consultancy's receivables.

Professional liability is also economics. The wholesale price must fund product-security errors-and-omissions cover if available, external legal review of the master subcontract, secure evidence storage, independent peer review for high-severity findings and a cap on aggregate liability. An unlimited indemnity, a conformity warranty, or liability tied to the end customer's machine revenue can make a €7,000 job existential. If a credible partner will buy only on those terms, the model fails rather than “needs better negotiation.”

From service to company

The first partner is co-delivery. The founder writes the report, joins calls and observes every translation between manufacturer, partner and back office. The accumulating assets are a data-handling protocol, responsibility matrix, evidence schema, reusable analysis environment, issue language and a record of estimation versus actual hours. There is no white-label magic yet.

The fifth partner gets a portal and enablement kit: opportunity qualification, scoping calculator, statement-of-work modules, secure intake, status, questions, approval gates and branded outputs. One delivery lead can support several partners because intake and evidence formats converge. Every partner certifies two people on the process; every quarter, one sample engagement is audited. The founder appears only for new product architectures, high-severity decisions and partner business reviews.

The twentieth end customer, not twentieth signed partner, is the meaningful scale point. Analysts perform component and evidence work from playbooks; senior engineers review exceptions; the partner runs meetings. The reusable asset is not the customer's SBOM. It is the mapping from common industrial architectures and evidence patterns to review steps, plus a benchmark of hours, questions and failure modes. Customer artefacts remain segregated, exportable and deletable under contract.

Acquisition repeats only when enabled partners originate qualified work without founder prospecting. Delivery is teachable only when another reviewer can reproduce a finding and the partner can explain it. Value recurs because components, releases, vulnerabilities and support state change. The asset accumulates only when the next engagement becomes faster without reusing confidential customer material. If quality requires the founder on every client call, the channel has multiplied meetings, not capacity.

The partner-removal defence must be operational. Do not withhold knowledge; that destroys trust. Instead remain useful through maintained analysis environments, cross-client pattern knowledge, surge capacity, independent review, insurer-friendly controls, and continuously updated mappings. Give the partner an exit pack and customer-data export. A partner that stays only because departure is painful will eventually leave angrily; a partner that stays because the back office is cheaper and safer than rebuilding may compound.

The incumbent gets a turn

The most dangerous competitor may become the partner's preferred supplier on day one. Cybellum already combines SBOM, vulnerability, incident-response and evidence capabilities in a manufacturer platform and openly recruits solution providers. ONEKEY combines product analysis, monitoring, CRA workflow and consulting. A partner can resell a platform, attach its own hours and retain more client visibility than a labour-heavy back office allows.

Large test and assurance firms, specialist security consultancies, embedded suppliers and internal product-security hires are substitutes. The free or inexpensive layer—SBOM formats, scanners, issue trackers and document stores—can cover mechanical tasks. Meanwhile CEN and CENELEC are advancing horizontal and industrial CRA standards through 2026. Better-defined review steps reduce training time for this company and for every partner that wants to internalise it.

Channel conflict has four forms. First, the back office approaches the end customer directly. Second, two partners bring the same account. Third, the specialist's platform or another vendor starts selling services to the partner's customer. Fourth, the partner reuses the method with a cheaper subcontractor. The master agreement needs account registration with an expiry, non-solicitation limited in time and scope, rules for inbound end-customer requests, named ownership of derived templates, and a clean exit. These are contract design requirements, not claims that every clause will be enforceable; Dutch counsel must decide the text.

Trust is harder than contract. The partner sees the back office's errors before it sees its brand value. The end manufacturer may refuse to upload firmware to an unknown third party or require the actual provider's credentials. In the adjacent Hard2bit model, the vendor explicitly says credentials remain its own and some engagements should switch to direct contracting when the end contract requires them. Product security will produce the same collision. A disclosed specialist is sometimes the honest design, even if it weakens the “white-label” pitch.

The response is not to promise exclusivity across Benelux. It is to make partner success measurable: protected account, wholesale rate card, response SLA, two trained partner leads, joint quality review, and a six-month minimum once an end customer starts recurring operations. If a partner refuses training, controlled client access and responsibility boundaries while demanding a deep discount, it is shopping for invisible liability capacity, not building a channel.

The capable failure

The company recruits five respected consultancies, never poaches an account, delivers under their templates and earns excellent client feedback. The first year teaches the partners exactly how to scope the work and which parts require scarce judgement. One partner hires a product-security lead and keeps the analysts it already employs. A second standardises on Cybellum or ONEKEY and uses the vendor's professional services. A third keeps the back office only for peaks, then demands a 25% price cut because it owns every customer and can redirect the pipeline.

The supplier did everything a good channel partner should do: it enabled the channel. That enablement destroyed its information advantage. Its remaining capability—senior exception review—is valuable but episodic, while routine work moves in-house. Customer concentration makes refusal expensive, professional liability remains downstream, and no end customer knows the supplier's name. The company becomes a subcontracting practice with worse pricing power than direct consulting. Good execution loses because the channel captures learning, relationship and margin faster than the back office accumulates a defensible asset.

The first ninety days

  1. Days 1–15: choose one partner segment—small CE/machinery-safety advisers—and interview ten practice leads about the last product-security request they declined, referred or delivered badly. Record end-customer type, quoted price, delivery gap, liability objection and whether an actual opportunity remains open.
  2. Days 16–30: have Dutch counsel review a short master subcontract covering manufacturer decisions, confidentiality, account registration, non-solicitation, artefact ownership, incident escalation, liability cap, insurance, payment, audit and exit. Obtain an insurance indication. Spend no more than €7,500 before partner payment, including counsel, insurance advice, travel and secure prototype.
  3. Days 31–45: offer the €3,000 paid rehearsal to five qualified partners. Show the same wholesale card and redacted pack. No unpaid bespoke proposals and no “partner” logo announcement. A partner qualifies only by paying or placing a live end-customer opportunity into scoping.
  4. Days 46–75: deliver two rehearsals and attempt one live €7,000 wholesale baseline, 50% on booking. Work co-branded or as a disclosed subcontractor. Log partner hours, back-office hours, questions delayed by the communication chain, rework, security objections and the retail-to-wholesale spread.
  5. Days 76–90: offer the end customer, through the partner, a six-month recurring unit at €2,400 wholesale per month. The pass rule is two paid rehearsals, one paid live baseline and one partner-backed recurring proposal with at least 35% model gross margin for the partner and 55% for the back office. The kill rule is no live paid baseline after five qualified offers, any required unlimited liability/compliance warranty, or partners consistently withholding the technical access needed for safe delivery.

Verdict

TEST THROUGH A PARTNER

The assumption most able to reverse it is that an enabled partner will keep buying recurring specialist capacity instead of internalising the routine work and retaining the full margin.
